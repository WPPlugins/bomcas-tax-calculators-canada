## What this estimate is

This estimate is the year’s take-home pay on an Alberta salary. Alberta’s basic personal amount is {bpa:AB}. There is no provincial sales tax to fold into the net.

The annual table starts at {rate:AB.income.first}. Employee pension contributions use {rate:CPP.employee} up to {money:CPP.ympe}, except where the province uses the Quebec Pension Plan instead. Employment insurance uses the employee rate. A claim code, a taxable benefit, and a bonus are not in the sample. This is not a pay stub from the payroll page. The payroll page starts from one gross cheque. This page starts from a yearly salary.

## How the calculation works

Income tax for the year is estimated on the salary. Only the enhanced portion of CPP or QPP is deductible. EI premiums are not deductible. The employee pension contribution and EI still come off the cheque. The amount left is the estimated net for the year. Dividing that net by the number of pays is a way to picture it. It is not the remittance and it is not the tax withheld on the payroll sample, because the inputs are different.

The supplemental credit in the income-tax file is part of the annual tax, not a line you add after the net. Do not subtract a sales tax Alberta does not charge.

## What is left out

The Canada child benefit, the GST credit, and provincial benefit programs are not added. A spouse, tuition, and donations are not assumed. Employer health tax, where a province charges it, is the employer’s levy. It is not subtracted again from this employee net.

## Common mistakes

Reading {example:AB.salary.64000.net} as the paycheque on the payroll page. Those pages use different inputs. Quoting the 26-pay slice as the tax. Forgetting that a bonus or a taxable benefit would change the year. Comparing the net with a Quebec payroll net. The Quebec payroll sample does not produce a net.

## Questions


### Is this the pay stub?

No. The payroll page estimates one gross cheque. This page starts from a yearly salary of {example:AB.salary.64000.amount} and a net of {example:AB.salary.64000.net}.

### Is sales tax in the net?

No. Alberta does not levy a provincial sales tax, and this screen would not add one if it did. Sales tax is not take-home pay.

### Why is the higher salary not taxed in proportion?

Because the brackets rise. The higher salary’s tax is {example:AB.salary.112000.tax}.

### Are benefits included?

No. A taxable benefit you did not enter is not in the salary.

### Does the 26-pay figure include the employer’s CPP?

No. It is the employee’s net, split for illustration. The employer’s share is not part of take-home pay.

### Is this a filing?

No. It is a {year_label} estimate, checked {updated}.
