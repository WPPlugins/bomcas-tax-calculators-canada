## What this estimate is

This is the year’s net on a territorial salary. The basic amount is {bpa:NT}. Northern deductions the form does not ask for are omitted. GST is not a salary deduction.

The annual table starts at {rate:NT.income.first}. Employee pension contributions use {rate:CPP.employee} up to {money:CPP.ympe}, except where the province uses the Quebec Pension Plan instead. Employment insurance uses the employee rate. A claim code, a taxable benefit, and a bonus are not in the sample. This is not a pay stub from the payroll page. The payroll page starts from one gross cheque. This page starts from a yearly salary.

## How the calculation works

Income tax for the year is estimated on the salary. Only the enhanced portion of CPP or QPP is deductible. EI premiums are not deductible. The employee pension contribution and EI still come off the cheque. The amount left is the estimated net for the year. Dividing that net by the number of pays is a way to picture it. It is not the remittance and it is not the tax withheld on the payroll sample, because the inputs are different.

Do not switch in Nunavut’s basic amount of {bpa:NU} and expect this net to stand.

## What is left out

The Canada child benefit, the GST credit, and provincial benefit programs are not added. A spouse, tuition, and donations are not assumed. Employer health tax, where a province charges it, is the employer’s levy. It is not subtracted again from this employee net.

## Common mistakes

Reading {example:NT.salary.64000.net} as the paycheque on the payroll page. Those pages use different inputs. Quoting the 26-pay slice as the tax. Forgetting that a bonus or a taxable benefit would change the year. Comparing the net with a Quebec payroll net. The Quebec payroll sample does not produce a net.
