## What this tax is

An Ontario dealer collects harmonized tax on the price. There is not a second retail sales tax on that dealer invoice. A private sale is a different tax base. Toronto’s land transfer tax has nothing to do with a car.

The worked lines are dealer sales with the trade-in box left off. Title, registration, and a documentation fee are not inside the tax. A luxury tax imposed by another statute is not added.

## How the calculation works

Start with the dealer price before tax. The form applies the federal tax and the provincial or territorial vehicle tax and adds them. A trade-in reduces the taxed price only after you say it qualifies. General merchandise on the sales-tax page is not this invoice.

## Private sales and exclusions

A private sale uses the greater of the price and the wholesale value. A trade-in does not reduce that private base. The dealer examples do use a trade-in only if you turn the box on. They are shown here with the box off. Do not apply the private greater-of rule to the dealer prices below.

## Common mistakes

Adding a retail-sales-tax line on top of harmonized tax on a dealer deal. Letting a trade-in reduce a private sale. Using Quebec’s estimated-value deduction on an Ontario private sale. Comparing the tax with British Columbia’s dealer tax without noticing the prices are both dealer prices and the statutes differ.

## Questions


### What is the dealer tax on the first price?

{example:ON.vehicle.31640.tax}. GST to the seller is {example:ON.vehicle.31640.gstToSeller}. The provincial or harmonized portion is {example:ON.vehicle.31640.provincial}.

### How does a private sale work?

Tax is on the greater of price and wholesale. A trade-in does not reduce that base.

### Is there a second RST on a dealer sale?

No. The dealer tax is the harmonized amount. Do not add another provincial tax on top.

### Is registration included?

No.

### Why mention Quebec?

A Quebec private sale uses an estimated value, not Ontario’s rule. The dealer taxes are also different systems.
