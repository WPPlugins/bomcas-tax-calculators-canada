## What this tax is

This page prices the tax on a vehicle bought from a dealer in British Columbia, with the trade-in box left off. GST is the federal portion. The provincial portion follows the vehicle rule, which is not always the same as general PST on a toaster or a chair. A private sale can use a different base when you switch the form. A zero-emission reduction and a vehicle imported from abroad are not calculated.

## How the calculation works

Start with the dealer price before tax. The form applies the federal tax and the British Columbia vehicle tax and adds them. Title, registration, and a documentation fee are not inside that tax. A trade-in reduces the taxed price only after you say the trade-in is eligible. The worked lines do not make that assumption.

General sales tax on other goods is a different question. Do not copy a merchandise total onto a vehicle invoice.

## Programs and exclusions

The PST bulletin on vehicles describes exemptions and the passenger-vehicle rules. This estimate does not decide whether a buyer qualifies for an exemption. A luxury tax imposed by a statute is a further amount. It is not added to {example:BC.vehicle.29400.tax}.

## Common mistakes

Using the general PST example from the sales-tax page as the tax on a car. Leaving the trade-in out of the conversation and then comparing the quote with {example:BC.vehicle.29400.tax}, which assumed no trade-in. Adding registration to the tax line when the form never asked for a registry fee.

## Questions

### Does the tax line include registration?

No. {example:BC.vehicle.29400.tax} is GST and the provincial vehicle tax. The registry fee is separate.

### What if I have a trade-in?

Turn the trade-in on and enter the allowance. Until you do, the worked price assumes there is none.

### Is a private sale the same?

No. A private sale can use another base. The worked lines are dealer sales.

### Is luxury tax included?

No. If a luxury tax applies, it is on top of {example:BC.vehicle.29400.tax}, and this page does not compute it.

### Can I use the Alberta result for a car I will register in British Columbia?

No. Alberta’s tax on {example:AB.vehicle.29400.price} is {example:AB.vehicle.29400.tax}. Registration in British Columbia follows British Columbia’s rules.
