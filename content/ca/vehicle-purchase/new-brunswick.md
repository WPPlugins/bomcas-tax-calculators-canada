## What this tax is

A dealer sale is harmonized tax, not a second provincial tax on top. A private purchase is taxed on fair value under the provincial vehicle rule. Nova Scotia’s sales-tax change date does not reprice this dealer sale.

The worked lines are dealer sales with the trade-in box left off. Title, registration, and a documentation fee are not inside the tax. A luxury tax imposed by another statute is not added.

## How the calculation works

Start with the dealer price before tax. The form applies the federal tax and the provincial or territorial vehicle tax and adds them. A trade-in reduces the taxed price only after you say it qualifies. General merchandise on the sales-tax page is not this invoice.

## Private sales and exclusions

If a private price is only a little under wholesale, the province’s rule can still use a floor. The screen applies that rule when you are on the private path. The worked prices below are dealer sales. Do not read them as fair-value private purchases.

## Common mistakes

Stacking a provincial tax on a dealer harmonized bill. Using Nova Scotia’s current dealer tax of {example:NS.vehicle.31920.tax} for a New Brunswick car. Adding registration to the tax line.
