## What this tax is

The dealer tax is GST. The territory does not add a provincial-style vehicle tax in this row, and it does not add a registration tax inside the tax line. A vehicle taxed in British Columbia is not this invoice.

The worked lines are dealer sales with the trade-in box left off. Title, registration, and a documentation fee are not inside the tax. A luxury tax imposed by another statute is not added.

## How the calculation works

Start with the dealer price before tax. The form applies the federal tax and the provincial or territorial vehicle tax and adds them. A trade-in reduces the taxed price only after you say it qualifies. General merchandise on the sales-tax page is not this invoice.

## Private sales and exclusions

Registration still exists as a registry fee. It is not hiding inside {example:NT.vehicle.29400.tax}. A private sale does not create a territorial sales tax the table omits. Nunavut’s dealer arithmetic matches on the sample and the pages are still not interchangeable, because income tax and residency are different.

## Common mistakes

Adding British Columbia’s provincial vehicle tax of {example:BC.vehicle.29400.provincial} because the car was shipped from the south. Using the Alberta page and ignoring that this is a territorial registry. Folding a plate fee into the GST.

## Questions


### What is the dealer tax?

{example:NT.vehicle.29400.tax}, which is GST of {example:NT.vehicle.29400.gstToSeller}. The territorial line is {example:NT.vehicle.29400.provincial}.

### Is registration in that figure?

No. The sales-tax table has no separate territorial registration tax.

### Should a southern PST be added?

Only if the vehicle is taxed in that province. British Columbia’s tax on this dealer price is {example:BC.vehicle.29400.tax}.

### Does a private sale add a territorial tax?

Not one this table left out.

### Why is Nunavut’s dealer tax the same on the sample?

Both charge GST only. The pages stay separate so the registry and the income-tax consequences are not blended.
