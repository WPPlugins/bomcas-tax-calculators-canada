## What this tax is

A dealer sale in Quebec can bear GST and QST. That is not Ontario harmonized tax and it is not the welcome tax on a building. A private sale follows the vehicle rule for the estimated value.

The worked lines are dealer sales with the trade-in box left off. Title, registration, and a documentation fee are not inside the tax. A luxury tax imposed by another statute is not added.

## How the calculation works

Start with the dealer price before tax. The form applies the federal tax and the provincial or territorial vehicle tax and adds them. A trade-in reduces the taxed price only after you say it qualifies. General merchandise on the sales-tax page is not this invoice.

## Private sales and exclusions

On a private sale the base uses the greater of the price and the estimated value. The estimated value starts from wholesale and takes the deduction the bulletin describes. GST on a sale to a non-registrant can be zero. The worked lines below are dealer sales, so they are not that private result. Related-party treatment stays off until you select it.

## Common mistakes

Using Ontario’s private rule, where a trade-in does not reduce the greater of price and wholesale, as if it were Quebec’s estimated value. Dropping QST because the form showed a GST amount. Adding the welcome tax to a car.

## Questions


### What is the dealer tax?

On the first dealer price it is {example:QC.vehicle.29400.tax}. GST to the seller is {example:QC.vehicle.29400.gstToSeller} and the provincial portion is {example:QC.vehicle.29400.provincial}.

### What is different about a private sale?

The base uses the greater of the price and the estimated value. GST to a non-registrant can be zero. These worked lines are dealer sales.

### Is this the welcome tax?

No. The welcome tax is on a transfer of an immovable.

### Does a trade-in apply in the example?

Only if you turn it on. The worked line leaves it off.

### Why not use the Ontario dealer tax?
