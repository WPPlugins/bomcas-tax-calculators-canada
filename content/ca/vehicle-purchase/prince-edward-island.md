## What this tax is

A dealer sale follows the harmonized rate of {rate:PE.sales.hst}. A private sale uses the greater of the price and the wholesale value. A family transfer that meets the conditions uses the stated price only, and that box starts unchecked. Nova Scotia’s rate change is not this rate.

The worked lines are dealer sales with the trade-in box left off. Title, registration, and a documentation fee are not inside the tax. A luxury tax imposed by another statute is not added.

## How the calculation works

Start with the dealer price before tax. The form applies the federal tax and the provincial or territorial vehicle tax and adds them. A trade-in reduces the taxed price only after you say it qualifies. General merchandise on the sales-tax page is not this invoice.

## Private sales and exclusions

The worked lines are dealer sales with no trade-in and no family-transfer box. Turning the family box on for a sale that is not a family transfer understates the private tax. Leaving it off for a transfer that qualifies overstates it.

## Common mistakes

Using Nova Scotia’s dealer tax of {example:NS.vehicle.31920.tax}. Applying the family-transfer price to an ordinary private sale. Adding registration to the tax.

## Questions


### What is the dealer tax?

{example:PE.vehicle.32200.tax} on the first dealer price.

### What is the private rule?

The greater of price and wholesale. A qualifying family transfer can use the stated price when you select that box. The box starts off.

### Does Nova Scotia’s cut apply?

No. The Island’s rate is {rate:PE.sales.hst}. The cut is dated {date:NS.sales.latest_from} and it is Nova Scotia’s.

### Is registration included?

No.

### How is New Brunswick’s private sale different?

New Brunswick describes the base as fair value, with a floor when the price is close to wholesale. The Island’s test is the greater of price and wholesale.
