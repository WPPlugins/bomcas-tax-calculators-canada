## What instalments are

If your net tax owing is above the threshold, you pay income tax in instalments during the year instead of all of it on {deadline:balance_due}. The usual dates are {deadline:instalments}. This page estimates the federal instalment options from the net tax figures you type. It is not a payroll remittance and it is not GST.

Quebec administers its own instalments. The Quebec threshold in the firm file is not the federal threshold. Do not use one number for both.

## How the calculation works

The no-calculation option, the prior-year option, and the current-year option are the three ways people set instalments. You enter expected net tax for this year, last year, and the year before, plus CPP on self-employment and voluntary EI if they belong in the total. The form follows the option the numbers support. It does not read a CRA reminder.

A balance still due on {deadline:balance_due} is not erased because you also pay instalments. Instalments are prepayments of that balance.

## Worked examples

A person whose net tax stays under the threshold for the years the test uses does not get an instalment requirement from this estimate. Paying one anyway is a prepayment, not proof that the test was met.

A person who owed more last year than they expect this year can use the current-year option and pay less than last year’s reminder, if the figures you type are the ones the option requires. Understating this year’s tax to shrink the instalment creates a balance, and interest, on {deadline:balance_due}.

A self-employed person includes CPP payable on that income when the form asks for it. Leaving it out understates the total that the threshold tests.

## Common mistakes

Using a monthly paycheque as net tax owing. Ignoring {deadline:balance_due} because the return is not due until {deadline:file_self_employed}. Applying the federal threshold to Quebec tax.

## Questions

### Are the dates the same as payroll remittances?

No. Instalments fall on {deadline:instalments}. Payroll remittances follow the employer’s remitter type.

### Does a self-employed filing extension delay the tax?

No. The return may wait until {deadline:file_self_employed}. The balance is due {deadline:balance_due}.

### Which option should I pick?

The form shows the options from the net tax you enter. CRA’s reminder is the prior-year path. You may pay on a current-year estimate when that option is available and the estimate is honest.

### Is Quebec included automatically?

No. Quebec instalments are separate. Do not treat the federal threshold as Quebec’s.

### Is interest charged if I underpay?

Yes, on the shortfall, under the instalment interest rules. This page shows the payment, not a waiver.
