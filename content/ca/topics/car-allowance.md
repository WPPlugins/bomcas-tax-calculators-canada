## What the allowance is

An employer may pay a per-kilometre allowance for business driving. If the rate is no higher than the prescribed rate, the allowance can stay off the employee’s income. A higher rate, or a flat monthly amount that is not based on kilometres, can be a taxable benefit. This page applies the prescribed rates in the {year_label} file. It does not decide a dispute about whether a trip was business travel.

Provinces share one pair of rates: a higher rate for the first block of kilometres and a lower rate after that. Yukon, the Northwest Territories, and Nunavut use a higher pair. The form asks you to say when the travel is in a territory.

## How the calculation works

Business kilometres are split at the threshold in the rate file. The first block uses the higher cents-per-kilometre rate. The rest uses the lower rate. Territory travel uses the territory pair instead. The result is the allowance the prescribed rates support. An amount the employer paid above that is the part to look at as a benefit. The form does not gross up a benefit for CPP.

## Worked examples

A short year of business driving stays inside the first block and uses only the higher rate. Adding personal kilometres does not increase the allowance. Only business kilometres count.

A long year crosses into the second block. The later kilometres are reimbursed at the lower rate. Using the higher rate on every kilometre overstates the prescribed amount.

Territory driving uses the territory pair even if the employee lives in a province. The switch is about where the travel is, not the address on the T4, when the form’s question is the one you answer.

## Common mistakes

Including the commute. Using the province pair for driving in Nunavut. Treating a flat car allowance as automatically tax-free because some per-kilometre allowances are.

## Questions

### Are the provinces different from each other?

No. The provinces share one pair. The three territories share a higher pair.

### Is the drive to work included?

No. The kilometres are business kilometres, not the commute.

### What if the employer pays more than the prescribed rate?

The excess can be a taxable benefit. This page shows the prescribed amount so you can see the gap.

### Does the allowance create RRSP room?

An excluded allowance is not employment income and does not create room. A taxable benefit can. This form does not compute the room.

### Where do the rates come from?

The {year_label} automobile allowance rates in the file, checked {updated}.
