## What CCA is

Capital cost allowance is the deduction for the cost of depreciable property. You claim it by class, not by deducting the whole invoice in the year you buy, unless a rule in the file says the class is treated that way. This page uses the class rates stored in the file. Classes that are not offered, including the ones the file names as excluded, are not given a made-up rate.

## How the calculation works

The undepreciated capital cost is the pool. A net addition can be reduced by the half-year rule, or the half-year rule can be suspended, according to the years the accelerated-investment note in the file describes. The rate for the class is then applied. A disposal reduces the pool by the lesser of cost and proceeds, in the way the usual rules work. The form shows the pool year by year. It does not prepare a schedule for a class you did not select.

## Worked examples

A class with a rate in the file, bought and available for use in a year the half-year rule still applies, deducts less in the first year than in a year when that rule is suspended. Using the full rate in a half-year year overstates the claim.

A later year applies the rate to the pool that remains, not to the original cost again. People who apply the rate to the invoice every year overstate CCA.

A sale in the first year reduces the addition before the rate is applied. Ignoring proceeds leaves a pool that is too high and can hide a recapture.

## Common mistakes

Deducting the full cost as an expense. Using a class rate from memory after the file has a different rate. Claiming CCA on land. Land is not depreciable. Claiming a class the screen does not list.

## Recent changes

The accelerated-investment note in the file describes the years the half-year rule is suspended and the year it returns. Those years are the file’s years, checked {updated}, not a slogan about “full expensing” for every asset.

## Questions

### Can I claim the whole cost this year?

Only if the class rules allow it. Otherwise the rate in the file applies to the pool, with the first-year rule the file describes for that year.

### What happens when I sell?

Proceeds reduce the pool, up to the original cost. Too little pool left can mean recapture. The form shows the pool. It does not give tax advice on a specific sale.

### Are all classes listed?

No. The screen lists the classes in the file. A missing class is not estimated at a neighbouring class’s rate.

### Does the province change the federal rate?

CCA class rates used here are the federal rates in the file. Provincial income tax may still apply to the income after the deduction. This page does not restate every provincial corporate rate.

### Is land included?

No. Land is not depreciable property.
