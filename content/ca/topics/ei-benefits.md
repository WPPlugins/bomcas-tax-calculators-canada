## What this benefit is

A regular employment-insurance benefit replaces part of insurable earnings when you are laid off and you qualify. The rate in the file is {rate:house.ei.benefit} of weekly insurable earnings. Weekly earnings cannot exceed the maximum that follows from annual insurable earnings of {money:house.ei.max}. This page does not decide whether you have enough hours, and it does not estimate sickness, maternity, parental, or caregiving benefits. Those have their own rules.

## How the calculation works

Insurable earnings are capped by the annual maximum of {money:house.ei.max}. Divide the insurable amount by 52 to see the weekly ceiling, then apply {rate:house.ei.benefit}. Earnings above the cap do not raise the benefit. A waiting period, if it applies to the claim, is not paid. The form does not look up your Record of Employment.

The premium you and your employer paid is a different number. The employee premium rate on a paycheque is {rate:EI.employee}. That premium is not the benefit rate.

## Worked examples

A worker at the earnings cap has the maximum weekly benefit the file allows: {rate:house.ei.benefit} of the weekly equivalent of {money:house.ei.max}. Earning more than the cap does not increase it.

A worker with lower insurable earnings receives {rate:house.ei.benefit} of those earnings, not the maximum. Using the maximum because a colleague received it overstates the claim.

A person who does not qualify, because hours or the reason for separation fail the test, receives nothing. The formula is not a promise that a claim will be approved.

## Common mistakes

Confusing the benefit rate of {rate:house.ei.benefit} with the premium rate of {rate:EI.employee}. Ignoring the cap of {money:house.ei.max}. Using this regular-benefit estimate for parental benefits.

## Recent changes

The maximum insurable earnings of {money:house.ei.max} and the benefit rate are the {year_label} figures checked {updated}. The maximum moves most years. Do not reuse last year’s cap.

## Questions

### Is every dollar of salary insured?

Only up to {money:house.ei.max}. Above that, the weekly benefit does not rise.

### What share is replaced?

{rate:house.ei.benefit} of weekly insurable earnings, for a regular benefit, subject to the cap.

### Does Quebec change this page?

Quebec parental benefits are a different program. This page is the regular EI benefit, not QPIP.

### Will CRA or Service Canada pay this amount because the form says so?

No. Qualification, the waiting period, and the reason for separation are decided on the claim. This is an estimate of the weekly amount if you qualify.

### Is the premium the same as the benefit?

No. You pay a premium at {rate:EI.employee} on insurable earnings. The benefit, if approved, is {rate:house.ei.benefit} of weekly insurable earnings.
