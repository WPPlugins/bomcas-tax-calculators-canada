## What the credit is

A gift to a qualifying charity can produce a non-refundable tax credit. It is not a deduction that simply comes off income, and it is not a matching grant. The federal credit is {rate:house.donations.first} on the first {money:house.donations.slice} of gifts and {rate:house.donations.rest} on the rest. Where the gift is taxed at the top federal rate, the rate file also carries an extra federal amount. The provincial credit uses that province’s lowest and highest income-tax rates, not a single national provincial rate.

## How the calculation works

The form asks for the gift and for taxable income so it can see whether the higher provincial rate is in reach. The first {money:house.donations.slice} is credited at {rate:house.donations.first}. The remainder of the gift is credited at {rate:house.donations.rest}, plus the extra federal piece when the file says it applies. The provincial piece follows the province you select. A gift in Ontario does not use British Columbia’s first rate of {rate:BC.income.first}.

The credit cannot exceed the tax it is claiming against, in the way non-refundable credits work. This estimate does not carry an unused gift forward. You would do that on the return.

## Worked examples

A gift at or under {money:house.donations.slice} uses only the first federal rate of {rate:house.donations.first}, plus the low provincial rate. There is no “rest” to credit at {rate:house.donations.rest}.

A gift above {money:house.donations.slice} splits. The first slice stays at the lower federal rate. Only the excess uses the higher federal rate. People who apply {rate:house.donations.rest} to the entire gift overstate the credit.

A person whose income never reaches the top band does not get the extra federal amount that belongs to income taxed at the top rate. Typing a large gift on a modest income does not create that extra.

## Common mistakes

Using the high rate on the whole gift. Using another province’s rate. Treating the credit as cash back that is larger than the tax otherwise payable. Entering a political contribution or a crowdfunding gift that is not a qualifying charitable gift.

## Questions

### Is the first slice always {money:house.donations.slice}?

Yes, in this rate file. Only gifts above that amount use {rate:house.donations.rest}.

### Does the province matter?

Yes, for the provincial credit. The federal rates are the same. Select the province that will tax the return.

### Can the credit be refunded if I owe no tax?

No. It is non-refundable. Unused gifts may be carried on the return. This form does not do the carry-forward.

### Is this the same as a political contribution credit?

No. Political contributions use a different credit. Do not enter them here.

### Why does income change the result?

The higher provincial rate, and the extra federal amount, depend on whether income reaches the top bracket. A gift alone does not put you there.
