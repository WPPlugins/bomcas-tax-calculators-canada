## What is being estimated

This page estimates tax on active business income of a Canadian-controlled private corporation, using the small-business rate and the general rate in the corporation file. It is not a T2. It does not apply the passive-income grind, the taxable-capital grind, or personal tax on the dividend you later take out. Salary versus dividend is a different screen.

## How the calculation works

You enter active business income and the amount you have already decided qualifies for the small-business deduction. The form does not decide that amount for you. Federal tax uses the federal small-business and general rates in the file. Provincial tax uses the province and the year. Where a province’s rate changes during the year, the file’s day-weighted segments are used. Ontario’s split is in the corporate file. It is not the British Columbia July payroll date.

Quebec’s small-business deduction depends on hour and sector tests the form does not perform. The checkbox says you have already done those tests. If you have not, leave it off. The screen will not pretend the full deduction applies.

## Worked examples

Income that you have determined is inside the small-business limit is taxed at the small-business rates. Income above the amount you marked as eligible is taxed at the general rates. Moving a dollar from one line to the other changes the tax by the gap between those rates, not by a personal bracket.

A taxation year that straddles a provincial rate change uses both segments in proportion to the days in the file. One rate for the whole year is wrong when the file shows two.

A corporation that is not a Canadian-controlled private corporation does not get this small-business deduction. Do not use the small-business line for it.

## Common mistakes

Letting the form decide the small-business limit. Using a personal tax rate on corporate profit. Importing the July payroll withholding rate into the corporate year. Turning on Quebec’s full deduction without the tests.

## Recent changes

Federal and provincial corporate rates are the ones in the file checked {updated}. A budget rate that is not in the file is not applied.

## Questions

### Does this include tax on a dividend to the owner?

No. The dividend is personal tax. Use the salary-versus-dividend screen for that comparison, and remember Quebec is off there.

### Who decides the small-business amount?

You do, from the limit and the grinds that apply. The form taxes the amount you enter.

### Why can Ontario show two rates in one year?

Because the file contains day-weighted segments. The year is not forced onto a single rate or onto a payroll date from another province.

### Is Quebec automatic?

No. The full small-business deduction is on only when you say the hour and sector tests are already satisfied.

### Is this a T2?

No. It is an estimate of the rates in the file for {year_label} as the file records them, checked {updated}.
