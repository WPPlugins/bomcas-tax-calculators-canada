## What is being estimated

If you file an individual return late and you owe tax, the late-filing penalty is a percentage of the balance owing, with a higher percentage when you were penalized in earlier years or the CRA demanded the return. Interest on the overdue balance uses the prescribed rate for the quarter. This page does not estimate a penalty on a return that has no balance owing.

The due date for a balance is {deadline:balance_due}. A self-employed filer may have until {deadline:file_self_employed} to file, but a balance is still due on the earlier date. Interest does not wait for the later filing date.

## How the calculation works

Enter the balance that was owing on the due date, the due date, the filing date, and the payment date. The penalty uses the number of months the return is late, within the statutory cap. The interest uses the prescribed quarterly rates in the rate file. A date that falls outside the quarters the file publishes does not receive an invented rate. Interest for that day is unavailable, not zero.

A demand to file, or a penalty in one of the three preceding years, raises the penalty. Those boxes are facts you set. The form does not know your history.

## Worked examples

A balance paid on {deadline:balance_due}, with the return filed on time, has no late-filing penalty and no arrears interest. The estimate is not a fee for filing.

A return filed after the due date, with a balance still unpaid, accrues the penalty on that balance and interest from the due date until you pay. Paying the tax but filing late can still leave the penalty. Filing on time but paying late leaves the interest.

A date in a quarter the file does not list stops the interest calculation. Do not type zero and treat the debt as frozen.

## Common mistakes

Using {deadline:file_self_employed} as the day interest starts. Interest on a balance starts at {deadline:balance_due}. Ignoring a prior penalty and understating the rate. Filling an unpublished quarter with the last rate you remember.

## Recent changes

The prescribed rates are the quarters stored in the file, checked {updated}. Each quarter has its own CRA page. This estimate follows the file, not a rate remembered from a news story.

## Questions

### When does interest start?

On {deadline:balance_due} for a balance that was due that day, even if you may file the return later because of self-employment.

### What if the quarter is not in the file?

Interest for that day is unavailable. It is not estimated at zero and it is not copied from another quarter.

### Does a zero balance still produce a penalty?

The late-filing penalty this page estimates is on the balance owing. A nil balance is not given a penalty here.

### Can a previous penalty change this year?

Yes. If a late-filing penalty was payable in one of the three preceding years, or the CRA demanded the return, the percentage is higher. Set those boxes only when they are true.

### Is this the prescribed rate on a refund CRA owes me?

No. This is arrears on tax you owe. Refund interest is a different rate and is not estimated here.
