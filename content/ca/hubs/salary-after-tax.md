## What these pages estimate

Each page starts with a yearly salary and estimates income tax. Only the enhanced portion of CPP is deductible. EI premiums are not. The employee pension contribution and EI still come off the cheque. The net is the year’s take-home pay. Dividing it by 26 is a picture of that net. It is not the cheque on the payroll page, which starts from a gross pay and a pay frequency.

Quebec uses the Quebec Pension Plan and the federal abatement. The other pages use the Canada Pension Plan. Ontario’s health premium and surtax are annual Ontario features. They are not a city tax and they are not copied onto a prairie salary.

## How to choose a page

Use the province or territory of residence for the salary. A job that moves does not get the average of two nets. Sales tax never comes off these totals. Nova Scotia’s harmonized-tax change does not raise take-home pay on this index, because sales tax was never in the subtraction.

The Quebec payroll sample can refuse a net. The Quebec salary page is still allowed to show a net on a yearly salary. Those two screens answer different questions. Do not paste one into the other.

## What the index does not do

It does not add a bonus, a taxable benefit, or a claim code you did not type. It does not pay the Canada child benefit. It does not subtract employer health tax from the employee.

## Questions

### Why doesn’t the net match the payroll page?

The inputs differ. One page is a yearly salary. The other is a single gross cheque.

### Is the 26-pay figure the remittance?

No. It is the employee’s net, split so you can see it. The employer remits on the remitter’s schedule.

### Does a higher salary get taxed in proportion?

No. The brackets are not flat. Read the second salary on the page you opened.

### Does Yukon phase out the basic amount on the sample salaries?

The phase-out starts at {money:YT.bpa.from}. The sample salaries on that page are below it.

### Is the take-home figure a pay stub?

No.
