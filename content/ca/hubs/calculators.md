## How to use this index

Each calculator answers one question for one place, or one federal program. Open the province where you live, where the payroll is, or where the sale or the property is taxed. Do not average two provinces. A person who moved during the year needs the residency rule, not a blend of the two results.

Each calculator answers one place or one federal program. Open that page and enter your own figures.

The arithmetic stays in the browser. A result is stored only if you later ask for an email or a PDF. The pages do not file a return and they do not replace a notice of assessment.

## What you will find

Income tax, sales tax, payroll, take-home pay, vehicle tax, and British Columbia and Ontario property transfer tax each have a page per province or, for transfer tax, per province that has a table. Federal programs, including the FHSA, RRSP room, the TFSA, the Canada child benefit, and capital gains, are single pages. They do not change their limits because of the province, except where the page says a province actually changes the tax, as capital gains and donations do.

## A note on the numbers

Every figure comes from the {year_label} rate file, checked {updated}. If a later budget changes a rate, the page keeps the checked figure until the file is updated.

## Questions

### Does the index calculate a combined bill?

No. Open one place or one program.

### Which year do these pages use?

They use {year_label}, the year in the rate file, checked {updated}.

### Where do worked examples come from?

From the same engines as the forms, for the wages, prices, and pays printed beside them.

### Can I rely on a page as a filing position?

No. Credits, residency, and exemptions the form does not ask for are omitted.

### Which year is this?

{year_label}, checked {updated}.
