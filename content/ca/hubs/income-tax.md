## What these pages estimate

Each page estimates federal income tax and the provincial or territorial income tax on employment income for a single person. The basic personal amount is the credit the form applies. A spouse, a dependant, tuition, medical expenses, and donations are left out. They are not filled in as zero.

A balance owing is due {deadline:balance_due}. A person who carried on a business may file by {deadline:file_self_employed}. The balance does not wait for that later date.

## How to choose a page

Open the province or territory where you are resident for the year. A move during the year is not the average of the two pages. Run each page only for the income that belongs to that residency, under the residency rules, instead of adding the two combined-tax figures.

Quebec is the page that applies the federal abatement. Ontario is the page with surtax and the health premium. British Columbia is the page with the tax reduction, and its July payroll rate is not the rate on the return. Manitoba phases the basic amount between two high thresholds. Yukon’s basic amount follows the federal amount. The territories do not share one table.

## What the index does not do

It does not add the provinces together. It does not file a return. It does not know days of residency, a second job, or a pension adjustment. Sales tax is not part of any of these results, including in the provinces that charge harmonized sales tax and in Alberta, where there is no provincial sales tax.

This index covers the Canadian provinces and territories in the rate file.

## Questions

### Which page do I open after a move?

The page for each place, for the income that belongs there. Do not average the two combined-tax figures.

### Why is Quebec’s federal tax lower on the same wage?

The Quebec abatement reduces basic federal tax. The other provinces do not receive it.

### Does British Columbia’s July rate belong on the return?

No. After {deadline:bc_payroll_july}, payroll withholding can use a different rate. The return uses the annual bands.

### Are benefits included?

No. The Canada child benefit and the GST credit are not in these totals.
