## What these pages estimate

British Columbia property transfer tax and Ontario land transfer tax are different statutes. Each page splits the price across that province’s bands. The whole price is not taxed at the top rate. The worked prices leave the first-time claim off.

Ontario’s page also leaves Toronto’s municipal land transfer tax off until you select it. If a price sits outside the municipal schedule the file will calculate, the Toronto amount is unavailable. Unavailable is not zero.

Quebec’s welcome tax is not estimated from these bands. It has its own calculator. The other provinces are not given a silent land-transfer result.

## How to use the two pages

Open British Columbia for a British Columbia registration and Ontario for an Ontario registration. A price that has moved from one province to the other is not the average of {example:BC.land.650000.tax} and {example:ON.land.650000.tax}. Those two figures are the same sticker price run through two tables, with the special boxes off.

A further tax on a high residential value, where the statute has one, applies only when you select it. A first-time refund applies only when the buyer qualifies.

## What the index does not do

It does not file the registration. It does not know a rebate you did not select. It does not invent a land-transfer tax for a province that has no table in this set.

## Questions

### Why only two provinces?

Because those are the two published tables. A missing province is not taxed at zero by this index.

### Is Toronto in the Ontario examples?

No. Select Toronto when the municipal tax applies. Otherwise the figure is the provincial table only.

### Is the welcome tax here?

No. Use the Quebec welcome-tax page. Its brackets are not these bands.

### Is the first-time claim assumed?

No. The worked prices leave it off.

### Which provinces have a transfer tax here?

British Columbia and Ontario. A province without a published table is not treated as zero.
