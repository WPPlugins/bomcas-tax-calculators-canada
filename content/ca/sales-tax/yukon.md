## What this tax is

Yukon does not levy a territorial sales tax. A standard taxable supply taxed in Yukon bears GST at {rate:YT.sales.gst}. British Columbia PST applies when the supply is taxed in British Columbia, not because the highway crosses the border. This page is a general supply. Yukon income tax, which mirrors the federal basic amount, is not a line on the receipt.

## How the calculation works

Multiply the pre-tax price by the GST rate. Provincial, harmonized, and Quebec components stay at zero. If you are pricing a supply that the place-of-supply rules tax in British Columbia, close this page and open the British Columbia page. Do not put half of British Columbia’s PST on a Yukon invoice.

## What is left out

The vehicle page prices a car. The sales table has no separate territorial registration tax. Exempt supplies are left exempt. A municipal charge in Whitehorse is not inferred.

## Common mistakes

Splitting the difference with British Columbia. Their tax on the first price is {example:BC.sales.2500.tax}. Yukon’s is {example:YT.sales.2500.tax}. Using Alberta’s page and ignoring that Yukon income tax is a different statute. Applying Nova Scotia’s HST date.

## Questions

### Does the British Columbia border add PST?

Only if the supply is taxed in British Columbia. This page’s provincial line is {example:YT.sales.2500.pst}.

### Is GST the whole tax?

Yes, for a standard supply taxed in Yukon. The first price’s tax is {example:YT.sales.2500.tax}.

### Should income tax go on the invoice?

No. Yukon income tax is calculated on taxable income, not on a shop price.

### Are cars included?

No. Use the vehicle page. Registration is extra.

### Is zero provincial tax an error?

No. The territory does not levy it.

### Is this a return?

No. It is the {year_label} table, checked {updated}.
