## What this tax is

The Northwest Territories levy income tax on taxable income. There is no territorial sales tax in the sales table, and that fact does not reduce this income tax. The estimate is employment income for a single person. Northern residence deductions and other claims the form does not ask for are left out.

The basic personal amount is {bpa:NT}. The credit rate is {rate:NT.income.credit}. A balance is due {deadline:balance_due}. The business filing date {deadline:file_self_employed} does not move the balance.

## How the calculation works

Only the enhanced portion of CPP comes off the wage. EI premiums are not deductible. Federal tax and territorial tax are calculated on what remains. Territorial tax starts at {rate:NT.income.first} and the top rate is {rate:NT.income.top}. The basic amount is the territorial figure, not Nunavut’s and not the federal amount.

## Worked examples

A wage of {example:NT.income.42000.employment} produces territorial tax of {example:NT.income.42000.provincialTax} and federal tax of {example:NT.income.42000.federalTax}. Combined tax is {example:NT.income.42000.totalTax}. The territorial basic amount on the line is {example:NT.income.42000.provincialBpa}.

A wage of {example:NT.income.86000.employment} produces combined tax of {example:NT.income.86000.totalTax}. The extra tax is the cost of the higher bands, not a sales tax that the territory does not levy.

A wage of {example:NT.income.155000.employment} produces combined tax of {example:NT.income.155000.totalTax}. The top rate of {rate:NT.income.top} applies to the top slice only.

## Credits and programs

The basic personal amount is the credit in this estimate. The territorial cost-of-living and residence amounts are not calculated, because the form does not ask where in the territory you lived or for how many days. Do not assume they are hidden inside {example:NT.income.42000.totalTax}.

## Common mistakes

Using Nunavut’s basic amount of {bpa:NU}. Adding a provincial sales tax the territory does not charge. Treating the result as if a northern deduction had already been taken. Reading the amount left as a paycheque.

## Neighbouring jurisdictions

Nunavut’s first rate is {rate:NU.income.first} and its basic amount is {bpa:NU}. On the first wage, Nunavut’s combined tax is {example:NU.income.42000.totalTax}, lower than the {example:NT.income.42000.totalTax} on this page. The two territories are not interchangeable.

Yukon’s basic amount follows the federal amount, {bpa:YT}, including a phase-out the Northwest Territories row does not use. Yukon’s combined tax on the same wage is {example:YT.income.42000.totalTax}.

## Questions

### Is there territorial sales tax to add?

No. The sales table has GST only. Income tax is still charged. The first wage’s combined income tax is {example:NT.income.42000.totalTax}.

### Are northern deductions included?

No. The form does not ask for them. They are not buried in the basic amount of {bpa:NT}.

### Is this the same table as Nunavut?

No. Nunavut starts at {rate:NU.income.first} with a basic amount of {bpa:NU}.

### Does the top rate cover everything?

No. It is {rate:NT.income.top} on the top slice.

### Can I file from this estimate?

No. It is a {year_label} estimate, checked {updated}.
